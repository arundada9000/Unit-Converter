Micro-Time: Beyond the Second

Modern technology requires granular precision well below what a human can manually track. Our Time Converter natively supports the following microscopic increments:

  • Millisecond (ms): One-thousandth of a second. Standard measurement for internet latency (ping) and shutter speeds on cameras.
  • Microsecond (μs): One-millionth of a second. Essential in microprocessor clock cycles and high-frequency algorithmic trading.
  • Nanosecond (ns): One-billionth of a second. Used by physicists and computer hardware architects. Light travels roughly one foot per nanosecond.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How many hours are in exactly one week?

A standard week possesses precisely 168 hours (24 hours per day multiplied by 7 days).

What is a Fortnight?

A fortnight is a unit of time equal to exactly 14 days, or two weeks. Originally derived from the Old English term "fēowertyne niht" (fourteen nights), it remains frequently used in British English today, especially for payroll and standard holiday scheduling.

Why do leap years exist?

The time it takes Earth to completely orbit the sun isn't exactly 365 days—it is approximately 365.24 days. To keep our calendar aligned with the astronomical seasons, we inject one extra "leap day" (February 29th) into the calendar approximately every four years.
