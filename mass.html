Mass vs. Weight: What's the Difference?

In everyday language, "mass" and "weight" are used interchangeably, but scientifically, they represent different concepts:

  • Mass: A measurement of how much matter an object contains. Mass remains constant regardless of where you are in the universe. It is generally measured in Kilograms or Grams.
  • Weight: A measurement of the gravitational force pulling on that mass. Your weight changes depending on gravity (you weigh less on the moon!). However, for terrestrial purposes, weighing scales display mass equivalent measurements like Pounds and Kilograms.

Specialized Units of Mass

We don't just calculate basic groceries. Our tool spans across highly specialized fields:

  • Carat (ct): Predominantly used in gemology to measure the mass of diamonds and precious stones. One carat is equal to exactly 200 milligrams (0.2 grams).
  • Grain (gr): Originally based on the mass of a single seed of cereal. It is widely used today to measure the mass of bullets, gunpowder, and archery arrows. One grain is exactly 64.79891 milligrams.
  • Troy Ounce: Often used for precious metals like gold and silver. Note that a Troy Ounce is slightly heavier than a standard Avoirdupois Ounce.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How many pounds make up one Stone?

A "Stone" is a traditional British unit of weight. Exactly 14 pounds make up one Stone. If someone weighs 10 Stone, they weigh 140 pounds.

What is the difference between a Short Ton and a Long Ton?

This is a common source of industrial confusion! A Short Ton (US) is perfectly equal to 2,000 pounds. A Long Ton (UK) is equal to 2,240 pounds. The metric Tonne (often called a metric ton) is equal to 1,000 kilograms (approximately 2,204.6 pounds).

How accurately does the converter measure micro-units?

Our algorithm supports precision down to Micrograms (mcg), which is one-millionth of a gram. This level of accuracy is actively used in medical pharmacology for dosage distributions.
